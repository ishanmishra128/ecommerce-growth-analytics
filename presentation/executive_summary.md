# Executive Summary: E-commerce Funnel & Conversion Analysis

**Prepared for:** VP of Product · Director of E-commerce · Director of Growth Marketing · Director of Finance
**Period covered:** November 1, 2020 – January 31, 2021
**Prepared by:** Ishan (Friday) Mishra

---

## The Question

Traffic and engagement have grown, but revenue hasn't kept pace. This analysis identifies whether the gap is driven by weak acquisition quality, poor on-site conversion, low repeat purchase behavior — and where leadership should focus first for the greatest return.

## The Answer, in One Line

**The biggest opportunity isn't acquiring more traffic or fixing checkout — it's what happens the moment a session lands on the site.** Three findings support this, in priority order.

---

## Finding 1: On-site engagement, not checkout, is the real leak

Of 360,129 sessions in the period, **142,407 (77.2%) never reach a product page.** This is, by a wide margin, the largest point of loss anywhere in the funnel — far larger than losses at cart or checkout, both of which convert at healthy rates once a shopper reaches them.

**Implication:** Investment aimed at "fixing checkout" will have limited impact on overall conversion. The highest-leverage fix is improving what a visitor sees and experiences in the first moments of a session — landing page relevance, load speed, merchandising visibility, and search/navigation clarity are the likely levers, and are recommended as the starting point for a focused UX investigation.

## Finding 2: The largest acquisition channel is not the best one

**Merch Store Referral** traffic converts at **4.8%** — several times higher than Google, the site's largest-volume channel, which converts at under 1%. This channel currently drives a comparatively small share of total sessions (19,234) relative to its quality.

**Implication:** A modest reallocation of acquisition investment toward channels like this — rather than simply pushing more spend into the largest existing channel — could lift blended conversion without requiring total traffic growth. Recommend a controlled test scaling Merch Store Referral (or channels behaving similarly) to validate whether conversion quality holds at higher volume.

## Finding 3: Repeat customers punch far above their weight

Repeat customers make up just **13.9%** of the buyer base but generate **24.9%** of total revenue — nearly double their proportional share. This is a small but highly valuable segment.

**Implication:** Retention investment (e.g., post-purchase engagement, loyalty incentives, win-back campaigns) has an outsized revenue ceiling relative to its cost, since even a modest increase in repeat rate compounds against an already high-value segment. Recommend retention be evaluated alongside acquisition spend when prioritizing the next quarter's growth budget, not treated as a secondary initiative.

---

## Recommended Priorities

1. **CONVERT** — Investigate and address on-site engagement friction between session start and product view; this is the single highest-volume opportunity in the funnel.
2. **OPTIMIZE** — Run a controlled scale-test on Merch Store Referral (or similarly high-converting, low-volume channels) before increasing spend on lower-converting, high-volume channels.
3. **RETAIN** — Fund retention initiatives with the same rigor as acquisition spend, given the outsized revenue return from the existing repeat-customer base.

---

## Methodology Note

This analysis is descriptive and diagnostic — it identifies where and how large the opportunities are, not a prescriptive UX redesign or media plan. It's built on GA4 behavioral event data with synthetic enrichment tables (channel spend, product margin, support/returns) layered in to approximate a realistic operating environment. Full technical methodology, data architecture, and known data limitations are documented in the accompanying [project README](../README.md).
